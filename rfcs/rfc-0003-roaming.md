# RFC-0003: Roaming between sovereign islands

**Status:** Draft · **Date:** 2026-09 · **Refines:** RFC-0001 D4 (federated identity, home key custody), RFC-0005 (unique IMSI blocks, realms, overlay keys, registry) · **Implements as:** WBS 3.3, tested by WBS 4.1

## Context

A subscriber provisioned by association A must be able to attach on association B's island — a family fleeing one valley for the next, a responder moving between deployments — **without A ever handing B the subscriber's long-term key K**, and without any central ResCCOM identity service (RFC-0001 D4). The question left open in [stack/federation/README.md](../stack/federation/README.md) was *home-routed authentication* versus *signed subscriber-set exchange*, and what happens when the home island is unreachable ("refugee mode").

Two facts from 3GPP make this tractable without inventing anything:

- Authentication never needs K at the visited network. The home network's HSS/UDM (ARPF) derives **authentication vectors** from K; the visited MME/AMF only ever consumes vectors. Home-routed authentication is how every commercial roaming relationship already works.
- Open5GS, unmodified, already routes authentication by subscriber identity: in 5G an NRF can advertise UDM/AUSF instances with **SUPI ranges** (the visited AMF discovers the *home* island's UDM/AUSF for that IMSI block); in 4G freeDiameter routes S6a by **realm**, and RFC-0005 gives every island a unique realm and a unique IMSI block. <!-- VERIFY: Open5GS NRF supiRanges-based selection for UDM/AUSF; freeDiameter realm-routing configuration (rt_default / routing entries) against a remote peer over WireGuard -->

## Decisions

### D1 — Home-routed authentication over the overlay; sessions and services stay local

The visited island authenticates a roaming subscriber by reaching the **home island's UDM/AUSF (5G) or HSS (4G) across the WireGuard overlay** (RFC-0005 D2 endpoints), using upstream's own identity-based routing (SUPI ranges / Diameter realms). K never leaves home. Everything after authentication — SMF/UPF session, breakout, DNS, library, chat, voice — is the **visited island's own** (RFC-0001 D5 offline-first applies to roamers too): a roaming user gets the services of the island they are standing on, not a tunnel home.

Rejected: signed subscriber-set exchange carrying K or OPc. It would make roaming survive home-island outages, at the price of the one guarantee this project makes to associations — their subscribers' keys are theirs alone.

**Amendment (2026-09-15, after WBS 3.3-c's negative finding):** the *mechanism* named above for 5G — same-PLMN islands with NRF SUPI-range routing — does not exist in Open5GS: neither the pinned 2.8.0 nor upstream `main` stores, matches, or selects by `supiRanges` (only generated model files mention it), so no upgrade fixes it, and CLAUDE.md forbids patching upstream. The decision therefore moves to the alternative this RFC already named: **every island is its own PLMN, and roaming is standard 3GPP inter-PLMN home-routed roaming through Open5GS's SEPP (N32) across the overlay.** Concretely: islands take **MCC 999 with a registry-allocated MNC each** (RFC-0005 D2, amended), the visited AMF reaches the home island's NRF/AUSF/UDM via the two islands' SEPPs — the exact topology upstream ships as its own roaming examples (`configs/examples/5gc-sepp*-999-70 / -001-01 / -315-010`) — and the overlay's forward filter narrows to the SEPP N32 port only, a cleaner boundary than exposing NRF/UDM. D1's substance is unchanged: K never leaves home, sessions and services stay local, allow-lists (D2) gate which islands' SEPPs may peer, and the partition policy (D3) applies identically. Costs accepted: the lab's shared-PLMN fixtures are replaced by distinct-PLMN ones; a roaming UE must be allowed onto a visited PLMN (a SIM/handset matter the playbook and sim-tools must cover); and MNC space under MCC 999 is finite (≈1000 islands) — a registry question, not an obstacle for years. 4G follows the same shape: distinct PLMNs give standard 3GPP Diameter realms (`epc.mnc<MNC>.mcc999.3gppnetwork.org`), which makes 3.3-d's realm routing the normal case rather than a special one.

**Follow-up (2026-09-15, WBS 3.3-c v2, full writeup in [stack/federation/TASKS.md](../stack/federation/TASKS.md)).** The mechanism this amendment names is confirmed real: SEPP's own N32-c security association genuinely establishes between two distinct-PLMN islands over the WireGuard overlay, live, both directions (`SEPP established` in each side's own log). What the lab cannot yet demonstrate end-to-end is a UE actually attaching through it — not a defect in this decision or in SEPP/N32, but a gap in UERANSIM (the `[SIM]` UE CLAUDE.md mandates for development): its own PLMN-selection code only ever attempts registration on a cell matching the UE's configured PLMN, never on a genuinely visited one, and no config or CLI override exists. `[HW]` RAN work (a real phone, which does implement standard PLMN selection) is the likely path to close this, once hardware is available — noted here for whoever picks that up next, not scheduled by this RFC.

**Follow-up 2 (2026-09-15, WBS 3.3-c v2b, full writeup in TASKS.md).** [SIM] progress on the UE-attach gap without waiting for `[HW]`: OpenAirInterface's nrUE (already an approved upstream, `stack/ran`'s 1.2-a(5G-alt) rig) has no PLMN-suitability check and does send a real roaming Registration Request with a correct home-PLMN SUCI — confirmed live, and B's AMF does correctly trigger SEPP-based home-routed discovery for it, not a PLMN-mismatch rejection. Two real lab-profile gaps found and fixed on the way (a missing port on Open5GS's own inter-PLMN NRF-discovery client, and no local DNS for the synthetic FQDN it constructs) get the request all the way to a real `200 OK` from the home island's own NRF — closer than this RFC's D1 amendment or its first follow-up got. What comes back empty (no AUSF candidate) is now the specific remaining gap, isolated to Open5GS's own discovery-forwarding implementation, not to anything this project renders or configures. Still not full end-to-end proof of D1 in this lab, but each iteration has narrowed *where* it stops, not just repeated that it does.

**Follow-up 3 (2026-09-16, WBS 3.3-c v2c, full writeup in TASKS.md).** Follow-up 2's "empty `200 OK`" was itself observed only via hand-edited config, restarted by hand — never through the finished tool. Running the same attempt through the actual, finished `two-island.sh roam --ue oai`, with a new debug-logging knob, found and fixed three real script/orchestration bugs unrelated to Open5GS itself (most importantly: `nrf`'s own already-running process never picks up a peer-only config change without an explicit restart the harness wasn't doing) — and once fixed, produced a *different, more precise* finding than Follow-up 2's: the request never reaches a genuine `200 OK`, or any HTTP response at all. The TCP connection between A's SEPP and A's NRF reaches ESTABLISHED on both ends (confirmed live via each container's own `/proc/net/tcp`), Open5GS's own SBI client reports successful dispatch, and NRF is demonstrably alive and servicing other NFs throughout — but no HTTP/2 exchange ever completes on that specific connection, indefinitely. This narrows the gap further (it's not a discovery-logic exclusion in `nnrf-handler.c`, as Follow-up 2's source reading anticipated — that code is never reached) but does not close it. Worth an upstream Open5GS issue; not fixable here without forking (CLAUDE.md). A later review re-run (2026-09-16, TASKS.md) on a healthy Docker host found the stall does **not** reproduce and withdrew the upstream-issue recommendation: the real, stable block is one hop later — A's AUSF/UDM never advertise an FQDN in their NRF profile (plain `core_net` IP:7777 only), so `ogs_sbi_fqdn_in_vplmn()` (`lib/sbi/path.c:805`) never says "foreign", and B's AMF dials A's AUSF directly instead of via SEPP — which the overlay's own forward filter then (correctly) blocks.

**Follow-up 4 (2026-09-16, WBS 3.3-c v2d, full writeup in TASKS.md) — the first full cross-island authentication and registration success in this project.** Render what upstream's own home-PLMN roaming example actually renders: NRF/AUSF/UDM/SMF/NSSF addressed by 3GPP FQDN as their primary SBI listener (not a plain `core_net` IP), `extra_hosts` resolving every FQDN on every core container (no DNS server in this lab), and `serving:` on AMF/SMF so the foreign-PLMN check has a home PLMN to compare against in the first place. Once a live cross-island hop actually exercised a *second* FQDN-addressed NF (not just NRF), a second, unanticipated gap surfaced and was fixed the same way: AUSF/UDM/SMF/NSSF's own listener needed upstream's real default port (80), not this project's own earlier simplification (explicit 7777) — SEPP's "arrived home" forwarding reconstructs the target URL from the bare FQDN with no port, so it always lands on the SBI client's own default. With both fixes in place: the roaming nrUE receives a real `Registration Accept`, A's AUSF logs genuine `AUTHENTICATION_SUCCESS` for the roaming subscriber, K never leaves A, and B's AMF's own home-routed discovery of A's AUSF/UDM/SMF all complete with the exact FQDNs this RFC's D1 amendment named. This settles D1's mechanism as real and working end-to-end for authentication and registration, not just N32-c establishment (Follow-up 1) — the biggest remaining item this document tracked.

**A genuinely new, distinct finding, immediately after: what's actually happening is 3GPP Home-Routed (HR) Roaming, not the Local Breakout (LBO) this decision specifies.** Open5GS's AMF sets `h_smf_uri` (which is what triggers full HR-roaming SM-context handling in SMF) unconditionally whenever the same `serving:`-driven foreign-PLMN check that makes the auth path work is true — the alternative, `lbo_roaming_allowed`, is per-subscriber data in the home island's subscriber document (`slice[].session[].lbo_roaming_allowed`; upstream roaming tutorial §2), which this project's subscriber tooling did not yet write — corrected by the 3.3-c v2d review, task 3.3-c v2e. Confirmed live: A's own SMF/UPF, not B's, answer the roaming subscriber's PDU session request and allocate its IP from A's own UE pool — a real, standards-compliant H-SMF/H-UPF anchor, not a stub. That needs an N9 (UPF↔UPF GTP-U) tunnel across the overlay to actually carry user-plane traffic, which this decision's own overlay forward-filter (D1 amendment, re-narrowed 3.3-c v2) deliberately never provisioned — only SEPP's N32 crosses. This is not a bug to route around; it's this decision colliding with an Open5GS default neither this RFC nor any task before v2d asked for, and it needs its own considered choice (force LBO if that's ever reachable from config; or treat HR as this project's actual roaming mode and give N9 the same explicit design pass N32 already got) before RFC-0003 D1's "sessions and services stay local" clause can be called settled rather than aspirational. Tracked as open question 5 below; next task is 3.3-c v2e.

**Follow-up 5 (2026-09-16, WBS 3.3-c v2e, full writeup in TASKS.md) — the mechanism (open question 5) is settled; live end-to-end proof is not, this session.** Local Breakout is a subscriber-tooling change, not an N9 design pass: `resccom-sim` now writes `lbo_roaming_allowed` (default `true`) into every subscriber's `slice[].session[]` document, with `sub add`/`sub edit --home-routed` to opt a subscriber into Home-Routed instead. Home-Routed therefore stays available per subscriber for an island that wants it; the overlay's forward filter is unchanged — it still carries only SEPP's N32, deliberately never N9, since D1's default path no longer needs it. Applied live to island A's real subscriber store (with no UE holding a session, per the documented `db sync` migration quirk) and re-verified against `stack/core/verify.sh`, clean. What this follow-up could **not** do this session is exercise the roaming data plane live end-to-end: `two-island.sh verify`'s own precondition failed reproducibly, before and after a genuine, VM-uptime-confirmed Docker Desktop restart, on a WireGuard overlay NAT-hairpin fault that live `wg show` counters trace to Docker Desktop's NAT hairpin poisoning WireGuard's endpoint roaming on **both** peers at once — each peer's endpoint for the other drifts to its own `services_net` gateway, so fixing one side relocates the black hole to the return path (review, 2026-09-16). A host-networking limitation of running both islands on one Docker Desktop VM, not a defect in this decision's mechanism or in anything this task rendered; the fix is a simulated shared-WAN bridge in the harness, a future task. Full evidence: `stack/federation/TASKS.md` 3.3-c v2e.

**Follow-up 6 (2026-09-16, WBS 3.3-c v2f, full writeup in TASKS.md) — the NAT-hairpin block is fixed and verified live, twice; a new blocker surfaces one hop further.** `stack/federation/compose.wan.yaml` gives both islands' `wg-overlay` containers a dedicated `wan_net` bridge (`10.200.0.0/24`, static addresses) instead of reaching each other through Docker Desktop's host-published ports — exactly the fix Follow-up 5 named and deferred. `two-island.sh verify` now passes `ALL CHECKS PASSED` both directions with no recurrence of the bilateral endpoint-drift symptom, on two independent runs. `two-island.sh roam --ue oai`, run twice, reproduced identically both times: the roaming nrUE receives a real `Registration Accept` with authentication forwarded home via SEPP (Follow-up 4's result, now reached through the fixed overlay rather than hand-verified state), and B's AMF correctly selects Local Breakout for the subscriber (`LBO[1]`, B's own local SMF endpoint set up) — Follow-up 5's `lbo_roaming_allowed` flag confirmed live for the first time. Both runs then fail at the very next step: 13–40ms after AMF contacts B's own local SMF, a fast, network-failure-free application-level `PDU session establishment reject`. So the overlay was real progress but not the *only* blocker this decision's "sessions and services stay local" clause needed cleared — B's own local SMF now rejects the session for a reason this task's evidence did not capture (its own log wasn't dumped by the harness and the container was gone before it could be inspected further). M3's live proof remains open.

**Follow-up 7 (2026-09-17, WBS 3.3-c v2g, full writeup in TASKS.md) — settled negative finding: Open5GS's own PCF, not this project's rendering, is what stops this clause.** A `two-island.sh --log-level debug` knob plus the v2f follow-up's SMF-log dump gave the exact cause on the first live run: B's own local PCF answers SMF's SM Policy Association request with a real `404 {"title":"[imsi-001010000099999:1] Cannot find SUPI in DB"}` (`../src/smf/gsm-sm.c:728`). `pcf.yaml.j2` renders `db_uri: mongodb://mongo/open5gs` — PCF's subscriber lookup is a direct connection to its own island's local MongoDB, never an SBI call through NRF discovery the way UDM/AUSF/SMF/NSSF's roaming-relevant calls are (those carry `target-plmn-list`/`requester-plmn-list` and route via SEPP, per v2d). There is no SBI hop for any rendering change to route through SEPP in the first place, and the roaming subscriber's document exists only in A's Mongo — exactly what this decision's own text requires ("K never leaves home"; by extension, the subscriber's identity and policy-relevant data are the home island's). This is Open5GS's own architecture: no N24 (Npcf, V-PCF↔H-PCF) roaming interface exists in the pinned `gradiant/open5gs:2.8.0`, nor upstream `main` (matches this RFC's 3.3-c precedent — CLAUDE.md forbids patching upstream, so this is documented, not worked around). Provisioning the roaming subscriber locally on B too would silence the 404, but it is not a fix — it quietly turns a "roaming" subscriber into D3's guest-provisioning mechanism, abandoning the premise D1 states. **RFC-0003 D1's home-routed-authentication clause is fully settled, live, reproducible** (v2d's authentication, this follow-up's Local Breakout selection at B's AMF). Its "sessions and services stay local" clause cannot complete against Open5GS's shipped PCF for a subscriber whose identity correctly stays home; closing it needs either an upstream Open5GS N24 implementation (not filed by this task) or reconsidering open question 5's Home-Routed alternative as this project's actual mode.

**Review of Follow-up 7 (2026-09-17).** Cause accepted, conclusion corrected: Open5GS's PCF reads only the subscriber's `slice[].session[]` policy from its local database (`lib/dbi/session.c`), never credentials, so a policy-only inbound-roamer record at the visited island satisfies it with K still at home — the visited-PCF policy a roaming agreement provides, not D3 guest provisioning. Upstream's own roaming examples never show this gap because all three share one Mongo. M3 is one provisioning step away: 3.3-c v2h (TASKS.md), which amends D1/D5 accordingly.

**Follow-up 8 (2026-09-17, WBS 3.3-c v2h, full writeup in TASKS.md) — MILESTONE M3 IS REACHED. D1 is proven in full, live, for 5G, `[SIM]` path.** The review's fix, built: `sim-tools` exports a roaming-enabled subscriber's policy only (no `security` field anywhere, refused on import by code), and `two-island.sh roam` imports it into the visited island before the UE attaches, marked so `db sync` never disturbs it. `./two-island.sh --log-level debug roam --ue oai`, run twice, both runs identical and passing every acceptance bullet on the first attempt each time: real `Registration Accept`, the roaming UE's address in the *visited* island's own UE pool (Local Breakout), the home island's SEPP forwarding both AUSF and UDM requests (K never left home — confirmed by the visited island's own AUSF/UDM logging nothing for the roaming subscriber), the visited island's own SMF/UPF anchoring the session with no `HR Roaming in V-SMF`, the home island's SMF/UPF silent, and a real breakout ping to the visited island's services subnet completing `0% packet loss` both runs. Both teardowns restored the home island's tree clean and left its real, already-running core untouched. This is the first time this RFC's D1 — both "home-routed authentication" and "sessions and services stay local" — has been demonstrated together, live, end to end. Scope: `[SIM]` (OpenAirInterface's nrUE, not a real phone; both islands as containers on one Docker host). `[HW]` roaming and 4G inter-realm roaming (3.3-d) remain open and are untouched by this result.

### D2 — Trust is a ceremony between associations, recorded in the registry

Island B authenticates A's subscribers only if B has **explicitly allow-listed A**: an association-to-association exchange of registry entries and overlay public keys (RFC-0005 D2), performed by humans, offline-capable, revocable. The allow-list is part of `island.yaml` (RFC-0006 D4 surfaces it as a policy toggle) and renders into the NRF/Diameter routing config. No island authenticates strangers by default; there is no global trust anchor.

### D3 — Partition behaviour: live sessions survive, fresh registrations do not, guest provisioning is the fallback — never key sharing

**Corrected 2026-09-17 (WBS 4.1, measured — replaces the "bounded vector caching" text below, which was an EPC-era framing that the M3 design and the pinned Open5GS do not implement).**

When the home island is unreachable, what a roaming subscriber on the visited island keeps is exactly what the visited island already holds for them, and nothing more:

1. **An established session survives.** The visited island holds no key for the roamer — only a policy-only inbound-roamer record (D1 amendment, 3.3-c v2h) — but a roamer who is already registered has a NAS security context at the visited AMF and a PDU session anchored at the visited island's own SMF/UPF (Local Breakout). Nothing about that session needs the home island. Open5GS's AMF (`src/amf/gmm-sm.c:1752`, v2.8.0) handles a registration request that arrives integrity-protected under a valid context (`SECURITY_CONTEXT_IS_VALID(amf_ue)`: security context available, no MAC failure, a key set identifier present) as a local registration *update* — periodic and mobility updates never re-contact home while the context is valid. There is no vector cache to size, and no configuration for one. Measured (`stack/federation/two-island.sh drill --ue oai`, 4.1): the established session kept passing user-plane traffic with the overlay stopped (breakout ping to the visited services subnet, 5/5 packets, 0% loss), WBS 4.1 run 2026-09-17.
2. **A fresh registration fails.** A phone that re-registers from scratch (power cycle, airplane mode, coverage loss) has no security context, and B holds no key with which to build one: the AMF's only path is `nausf-auth` at home, via SEPP, and the overlay is down. Measured: a fresh re-registration of the same roamer, overlay down, failed at the visited AMF — `[imsi-001010000099999:suci-…] Cannot receive SBI message` (status 504, `lib/sbi`), the home-routed `nausf-auth` request to the visited SEPP unanswerable with no live N32 to home — no PDU session, WBS 4.1 run 2026-09-17. This is the honest partition limit; it is stated here as measured, not designed, and it is the price of "K never leaves home".
3. **Guest provisioning.** The fallback for anyone who cannot be authenticated — home island unreachable, home island unknown, or no ResCCOM SIM at all — is a **guest identity issued by the visited association** through `sim-tools` (a local subscriber in the visited island's own IMSI block, local key, local rules — deliberately *not* a copy of the visitor's home identity, and deliberately not an inbound-roamer record, which needs the home island). Measured: a local guest subscriber in the visited island's own block (`resccom-sim sub add`, full local credentials) registered and broke out locally with the overlay still down (address from the visited UE pool, anchored by the visited SMF, breakout 0% loss), WBS 4.1 run 2026-09-17. This is what a village would do for arrivals anyway; the design just says so instead of pretending roaming can outlast the home island indefinitely.
4. **Recovery is automatic.** When the overlay returns, the original identity registers again through the home island as before, and the home island's own log holds both visits (D5). Measured: on restarting the visited `wg-overlay`, both SEPP N32 ports were reachable again 1s later and the original roamer re-registered through home 7s after the restart (breakout 0% loss), WBS 4.1 run 2026-09-17.

Rejected: an "emergency" flag that relaxes authentication. A network that authenticates nobody in a crisis is the network a fraudster wants. Also rejected, again: caching or sharing authentication vectors or keys at the visited island to extend roaming past the partition — the pinned Open5GS has no such mechanism to configure, and building one would move key material off the home island.

### D4 — Messaging federation: standard Matrix federation over the overlay

Homeservers use the island realm as server name (RFC-0005), federate over WireGuard, and are configured to tolerate long partitions (federation sender backoff, room retention set for multi-day gaps) <!-- VERIFY: Synapse federation retry/backoff limits under multi-day partitions -->. Store-and-forward bridging over DTN for messaging is WBS 4.2's question, not identity's; content-library replication likewise. A roaming user's *Matrix* identity stays on their home server — chat works only while the overlay is up, and the portal says so (RFC-0001 D8), unless they create a local account.

### D5 — What each side learns, said plainly

Home-routed authentication means the **home island learns that its subscriber is roaming, and where**; the **visited island learns the IMSI** and serves its traffic. Both are inherent to this design and to every commercial roaming relationship. Both are logged, and the log is association-held. The threat model ([docs/threat-model.md](../docs/threat-model.md)) carries this as an accepted disclosure, and the playbook must tell subscribers.

**Amendment (2026-09-17, WBS 3.3-c v2h): the visited island learns roamer IMSIs earlier than "at first attach."** Local Breakout PDU session establishment needs the visited island's PCF to hold a policy record for the roaming subscriber before they ever attach (v2g's review, from Open5GS's own `lib/dbi/session.c`) — so the home island exports a roaming-enabled subscriber's **policy only** (`slice[].session[]`: QoS, AMBR, `lbo_roaming_allowed` — never `security`, refused on import by code, not convention) and the visited island holds it as a marked inbound-roamer record from the moment it's provisioned, not from the moment of first attach. This is a real, additional disclosure beyond what D5's opening paragraph already named: the visited island now learns *which* of the home island's subscribers are roaming-enabled and may visit, at export time, whether or not any of them ever actually do. Accepted, with the same mitigation v2h's own design already applies: export only subscribers the home island's operator has marked roaming-enabled (`lbo_roaming_allowed`, not every subscriber by default), and never export or hold anything beyond that subscriber's session policy — no credential, no location history, no usage data. `sim-tools/README.md` "Inbound roaming" has the full mechanism.

## Consequences

- WBS 3.3 becomes concrete: overlay module (static WireGuard first, allow-list rendered from `island.yaml`), NRF/Diameter routing rendered by `island-init`, vector-cache configuration, guest-provisioning runbook in the playbook.
- WBS 4.1's two-island exercise is the test of D1–D3: attach on the visited island with the home reachable, drop the overlay, re-attach within the cached-vector budget, exhaust it, fall back to guest provisioning, restore the link, confirm the home island's roaming log.
- Islands use distinct PLMNs (D1 amendment); Open5GS's SEPP/N32 support is the implementation path. The shared-PLMN/SUPI-range route is closed by evidence (`stack/federation/two-island.sh roam`), kept only as the reproducible negative check.

## Open questions

1. **Settled for 5G, negative (2026-09-15, WBS 3.3-c, full writeup in [stack/federation/TASKS.md](../stack/federation/TASKS.md)).** SUPI-range NF discovery does not work against the pinned `gradiant/open5gs:2.8.0`: NRF silently drops `supiRanges` on persist (confirmed live, a raw NFProfile PUT/GET round-trip), a one-shot "static profile" doesn't survive NRF's own heartbeat-timeout, and AMF does not re-select an AUSF/UDM instance per subscriber identity even when a second, correctly-shaped candidate exists. `stack/federation/two-island.sh roam` reproduces this live, on demand. D1's home-routed model is not falsified by this — the upstream *mechanism* named to implement it is what's missing — but it means 3.3-c cannot ship as designed without either an upstream fix (out of this project's hands, CLAUDE.md "never fork upstreams") or the SEPP/N32 path below, which needs its own design pass and a move off the lab's shared-PLMN model first. 4G Diameter realm routing (3.3-d) is untested; re-scope it with this finding in mind rather than assuming the 4G path fares better.
2. Vector-cache policy: batch size vs. exposure if a visited node is stolen (cached vectors are bounded and per-subscriber, but the number should be a deliberate choice).
3. Whether allow-lists are bilateral by construction (A trusts B ⇔ B trusts A) or independent — independent is simpler and matches how associations actually behave.
4. Roaming for *emergency* app traffic (RFC-0004) when the visitor's PEMEA credentials belong to another island's AP — likely "the visited AP serves everyone on the island"; confirm against PEMEA's model.
5. **Settled by review (2026-09-16, WBS 3.3-c v2d → v2e).** Open5GS selects Home-Routed or Local Breakout per subscriber session from the home island's subscriber document: `slice[].session[].lbo_roaming_allowed` (`lib/dbi/session.c`, UDR → UDM SDM `DnnInfo.lboRoamingAllowed` → AMF `nsmf-build.c`); unset means Home-Routed, which is what v2d observed live (A's SMF/UPF anchored the session, breakout ping lost). D1's Local Breakout is therefore the subscriber tooling's job (`resccom-sim` writes the flag, default `true`; 3.3-c v2e), not an N9 design pass.
